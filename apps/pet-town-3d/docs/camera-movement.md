# Camera movement

The original player and selected companions use one camera safety pipeline. Mouse, keyboard and controller input continue to set desired yaw, pitch and distance. Temporary collision corrections never overwrite those requested values. Companion first-person placement uses the same final geometry validation, with its own head target and field of view.

## Geometry and query ownership

`src/player/camera-query/create-camera-queries.js` loads `@dimforge/rapier3d-compat` locally before built-in systems initialize. This JavaScript package calls its bundled WebAssembly runtime; there is no Rust process, network service or IPC boundary. Its isolated Rapier world has static colliders and no actor rigid bodies. The desktop CSP permits WebAssembly compilation through `wasm-unsafe-eval`.

The source inventory registers rendered terrain chunks, solid tagged prop material ranges, explicit moving wood/metal prop meshes, and individual trunk geometry/transforms. Garden fences use their actual boards and posts instead of an enclosing cylinder. Canopies, decorative cloth, fire shaders, decals and actors do not form camera walls. Geometry/attribute versions, transforms and membership detect edits; collider replacement updates Rapier's broadphase through a step of the isolated world.

Each preparation covers the requested boom, intro distance and previous camera position, independent of a contracted zoom. Recovery and visibility searches expand that region before querying candidates. Nearby frames retain the expanded region until the focus travels eight metres from its registration anchor; this avoids rebuilding colliders whenever recovery expands a contracted view. Geometry changes and removals still update that retained region. Cached bounds/descriptors and a synchronous solve scope avoid refreshing unchanged geometry for every query. The query API supplies sphere sweeps, penetration/containment checks, filtered visibility rays, revision counters and idempotent disposal.

## Complete pose and movement

1. Follow the subject and derive a small rail-aware pivot correction.
2. Sweep a sphere along the requested boom. Prefer bounded pitch assistance before contraction. Contract promptly; hold and ease outward recovery.
3. Compose intro distance, fixed third-person field of view and ground/water clearance. Walking bob, landing dip, speed FOV changes and idle orbit are disabled.
4. Compute a sphere enclosing every near-plane corner for the actual aspect ratio and field of view. Recover an initially overlapping pivot/eye within a registered region.
5. Validate the full desired pose. Sweep from the previous safe eye position to the new position; slide through checked contact segments rather than crossing a wall during orbit.
6. Check opaque line of sight from the actual camera to the subject. Try bounded yaw/lift alternatives with both boom and movement clearance. Within 0.8 metres of the animated head, widen the bounded search to side/opposite views and higher lifts. Require head clearance for candidates and ease comfortable recovery back to the requested pose. If necessary, move through a checked upward waypoint without changing the user's desired orbit. Impossible views remain labelled as visibility recovery.
7. Enforce water clearance again after recovery. A roof-blocked upward correction keeps a previous water-safe pose or takes a checked local escape. A wholly enclosed initial pose uses an explicit reset above the finite world.
8. Re-register final query coverage and reject any final solid overlap before committing the eye position. Aim from a stable body reference. Use the animated head for visibility and invalid near-plane recovery; normal framing uses a cached root-relative body point and the existing HUD bounds.

The continuity rule applies to normal movement. Teleport, companion/mode changes and initially invalid poses explicitly reset prior camera history. This avoids demanding a continuous path between unrelated subject locations.

Manual orbit holds priority across short mouse-event gaps. It releases retained visibility waypoints and pitch assistance and bypasses the extra jump-recovery spring; ordinary input damping and final swept collision checks remain active. When a wall blocks an orbit, the camera tries a shorter checked boom, then contracts along its previous boom to make room to turn. A close but visible manual view does not trigger an automatic search for a more distant angle. Movement uses the rendered camera direction, so obstruction recovery cannot reverse the meaning of WASD.

Unchanged camera inventories retain their identity across frames. Geometry, transforms, membership and terrain changes still invalidate them. Foliage fade materials compile with their fade variant during loading rather than recompiling when the player first enters trees. Automatic rendering quality now reduces cost below sustained 55 FPS and requires sustained 59 FPS before trying a higher tier; explicit quality overrides remain fixed.

## Comfortable jump movement

Ordinary jumps hold the camera's takeoff height and let the pet move vertically within the screen. A reference camera projects the cached model's body, head and feet against the available vertical viewport. Height follow begins when the projected subject approaches those bounds; returning inside the allowance does not continually recenter the view. The initial research allowance was a tuning starting point: the implementation uses the full available body-aware interval so ordinary jumps at the standard distance leave the view steady. Extremely close views prioritize the head when the full body cannot fit.

On landing, release the held height and follow the new terrain height through a spring capped at three metres per second. Long falls also leave the screen allowance and gradually pull height downward. Swimming and gliding retain their own height targets. Stable aim follows the cached body reference instead of recentering the animated head each frame. Framing uses one angular spring on the ground and in the air, capped at ten degrees per second. Its error is measured from the uncorrected view each frame, so an existing correction cannot feed back into the next target. Reaching a screen edge never bypasses smoothing. Only a genuinely invalid near-plane view can require immediate aiming.

A jump temporarily holds its starting constrained boom distance, so clearing a trunk in mid-air does not expand the view only to contract it again on landing. That hold expires 0.2 seconds after landing; explicit zoom bypasses it. Pitch assistance enters and releases gradually, and a bounded lower-pitch alternative can preserve distance beneath a roof when raising the view cannot. Solid boom contraction remains immediate.

Brief opaque head occlusion waits up to 0.12 seconds before searching for another view. Eye collisions, movement collisions and critical head proximity never wait. Visibility alternatives prefer keeping the current height; ordinary recovery is eased and capped at four metres per second. Critical escape routes persist until reached. Every eased recovery checks the target sphere and full movement path; if interpolation would put the eye within 0.8 metres of the head, a checked clear endpoint takes precedence. A retained eye must also preserve that head clearance. When no visible route exists, a collision-clear return still eases rather than snapping back.

Jump correction smoothing remains after the safety solve. Every accepted intermediate passes head visibility, head clearance, sphere overlap, pivot sweep and previous-eye movement checks. Final water and overlap checks follow all smoothing. Snaps, subject changes and return from first person reset held height, aim and recovery history. Requested orbit and zoom remain user-owned; horizontal movement auto-follow remains available.

The production-source inspection and rendered before/after comparison are recorded in `var/camera-motion-comfort/implement-details/` and `var/camera-motion-comfort/view.html`. Bounded geometry at 30/60/120 FPS reports no committed sphere overlaps or crossed movement segments. The standard-distance open jump has zero eye-height and pitch travel. Very close low-roof views can still crop the body and require substantial angle changes; impossible framing, partial occlusion and perceptual comfort are not certified by these checks. The follow-up investigation in `var/jump-jerk/` reproduces a real close-view jump in the town browser: the previous animated-head outer guard produced a 1.79-degree frame correction (111 degrees per second). Two complete close-view jump traces with the continuous angular spring stay within ten degrees per second and report zero overlaps. These checks cover tapped jumps near the spawn benches and street lights; held jumps on terrain steps and native companion gameplay still need wider acceptance.

## Vegetation visibility

Head/torso rays detect trunk-only occlusion as well as canopy occlusion. The existing local dither fade uses the camera-to-subject capsule, including the subject endpoint, and a short release hold. It changes presentation, not solid geometry. The original player does not clear a selected companion's fade. First-person mode releases third-person foliage fading.

## Diagnostics and limits

Append `?cameraDebug=1` in a development view to display camera state, requested/resolved zoom, final overlap count, contact owner, collider count and rolling solve p95. Diagnostics are opt-in, bounded and contain no companion identity. Add `&cameraTrace=1` to retain the last 600 final eye/quaternion, interpolated subject, grounded and framing samples in the diagnostics element’s `data-trace` attribute. Publication is limited to five times per second and requires `cameraDebug` too. `context.cameraQueries.stats` includes preparation and query counters. Source transforms track authored trunk geometry; shader-only wind deformation is not a physics mesh. Native companion follow/control and first-person modes received basic live coverage. Controller/touch feel, gliding, enclosed initial poses and scene-edit behavior still need dedicated live coverage. Dense grass/flowers, other actors and partial body occlusion by rocks are visual limits beyond the head/solid safety checks; zero overlap does not certify perfect composition.

Runtime evidence and the acceptance matrix for this change are stored in `var/camera-system/implement-details/`. A passing build is not evidence of a visually correct camera path.
